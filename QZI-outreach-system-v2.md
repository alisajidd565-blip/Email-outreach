# QZI Labs — Client Acquisition System v2

**Built:** 17 Sep 2026 · Replaces the v1 approach that produced ~0.2% reply rate over 1,000+ sends.

## What changed and why

v1 failed on four things at once: free Gmail sending, Drive links, a portfolio with a misspelled bottle label, and a $20 to $80 price that read as unserious. v2 fixes the infrastructure first, then leads with a free custom spec ad, which is possible only because production cost is effectively zero.

**Core strategic bet:** at zero marginal production cost, the cheapest thing you own is a finished ad. So stop asking brands to imagine their ad and start sending it to them.

---

## A. Positioning statement

> I make short cinematic fragrance ads built from a brand's existing product photos. No shoot, no studio, no crew, and no shipping bottles anywhere. Direction, editing, music and sound design are handled on my side. The brand gets a finished commercial cut for Reels, paid social and the product page.

What this is NOT: "I make AI videos." Nobody buys AI. They buy a finished ad.

Do not claim: increased sales, guaranteed reach, performance results, or clients you do not have.

## B. Primary offer

| Tier | What they get | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Spec preview | 15 second vertical cut of THEIR product, 720p, watermarked | Free |
| Single ad | 20 to 30 second ad, 9:16 + 1:1 + 16:9, 4K, 2 revisions, music + sound | $350 |
| Launch pack | 3 ads from one launch (hero, teaser, detail) | $900 |
| Retainer | 2 ads per month | $600/mo |

**First-client exception:** $200 for the first two paying clients in exchange for a written testimonial and permission to name them publicly. Say it is an introductory rate and why, so it does not become your permanent price.

**Why not $80:** a brand selling a $180 bottle reads a $30 to $80 ad as either a scam or as worthless. You are also competing with Fiverr at that number instead of with freelance video editors, where $300 to $500 is ordinary and credible.

## C. Ideal prospect profile

Commit to independent and niche perfume houses. Drop candles and soap.

**Strong signals (contact these):**
- 1 to 15 people, founder reachable and named on the site
- Sells direct from own site at $80+ per bottle
- Instagram active, 2k to 100k followers, posting stills and flat lays, little or no video
- Launched something in the last 60 days, or has a launch page live
- Brand has a narrative already: a story, a character, a place, a collection concept
- Runs Meta ads (check facebook.com/ads/library by brand name, free and definitive)

**Weak signals (skip these):**
- Retailers and resellers rather than brand owners
- Only contactable via a web form with no named person
- Already posting polished video weekly, meaning they have a team
- Large houses with in-house creative (Amouage, Clive Christian, anything LVMH)
- Sites that say "no longer accepting orders" or look abandoned

**Where to build the list:** your existing `QZI-prospect-list.csv` tier 1 rows are already researched and good. Plus Fragrantica brand pages, Basenotes directories, Indie Scent Library, Instagram hashtags #indieperfume #nicheperfume #artisanperfume, and the Meta Ad Library for brands already spending.

## E. Primary cold email

Subject lines rotate. Use the product name where you have one.

```
Subject: {Product name}, in motion
Subject: A 15 second cut for {Product name}
Subject: {Brand name} in video
```

```
Hi {First name},

{SPECIFIC OBSERVATION: one verified sentence about a launch, a collection,
a bottle, or what their product page currently looks like.}

{WHY VIDEO: one sentence connecting that observation to motion. Smoke, oil,
light, pour, texture, warmth. Something a still cannot carry.}

I make short cinematic fragrance ads from product photos, so there is no
shoot to arrange and nothing to ship. Direction, editing, music and sound
are handled on my side.

Two examples: qzilabs.com/work

If it is useful I will cut a 15 second version for {Product name} so you can
see it with your own bottle. No charge and no obligation. Want me to?

Ali Sajid
QZI Labs
qzilabs.com

Reply "no thanks" and I will not follow up.
```

**Rules:** no dashes. Under 120 words. No price in the first email. CTA is a yes or no question, because questions get answered and statements do not.

## H. Objection responses

**"How much do you charge?"**
> A single ad is $350. That covers a 20 to 30 second cut, vertical, square and widescreen versions, two rounds of revisions, and the music and sound. Three ads from one launch is $900. Happy to do a 15 second spec of {Product} first so you can judge the work before deciding.

**"Send your portfolio."**
> Here you go: qzilabs.com/work. The {X} one is closest to what I would do for {their product}. If you tell me which product you want to push next I will cut a short version of it this week.

**"We are not interested."**
> Understood, thanks for replying. I will leave it there. If video comes up around a future launch, the work is at qzilabs.com/work.

**"We already have a designer."**
> Makes sense. Most designers I work alongside do stills and packaging rather than motion, so if that is the split I can sit next to them rather than replace them. If it is useful I can do one spec cut and your designer can tell you whether it is worth it.

**"We do not want AI ads."**
> Fair. What I am selling is the finished ad, not the tool. Generation is one step, and direction, editing, sound and pacing are the rest. If the output looks like AI it has failed. Judge the spec cut and if it does not look like a real commercial, say so and I will drop it.

**"Can you make a sample?"**
> Yes. Send me your best two or three product photos and tell me which product you want to push. 15 second vertical cut back to you within {X} days, watermarked, no charge. If you want it you pay and I send the clean 4K masters.

**"We will contact you later."**
> Sounds good. Is there a launch coming up that I should time it around?

*(Always end a soft no with a question. It gives them something easy to answer.)*

## H2. Closing rules (added 17 Sep after reading four real threads)

Four prospects replied warmly and all four died after the reply, not before it. The
outreach was never the problem. These are the rules that come directly out of those
threads.

**1. Answer the exact question asked, in the first line.**
Mona asked "can it look realistic enough for KIERIN" and got a car commercial. Naz asked
"how much is an Instagram ad" and got a $450/month Meta budget. Answer first, add context
after, never the reverse.

**2. Never move the number. Move the scope.**
Across four threads the price went $80, $60, $50, $40, $20, $10, free. Nobody ever pushed
back hard. If someone hesitates: "I could do a 10 second cut for $40 instead." Never
"okay, $50 then."

**3. "How much?" from someone who just praised the work is a closing question.**
Answer it in one line with a number and a next step. Do not discount, do not offer to
negotiate, do not send a proposal nobody asked for.

**4. Give things away BEFORE the price comes up, never after.**
A free spec ad before they ask the price builds trust. The same gift three minutes after
they ask the price is a discount to zero, and it removes their reason to pay.

**5. Answer the location question straight.**
"Where are you located?" killed the biggest thread. Being in Pakistan is not a problem.
Sounding evasive about it is. "I'm in Lahore and work with brands in the UK, US and
Australia" is a complete answer.

**6. Be payable.**
Never send a raw personal IBAN. Set up Wise and Payoneer so a client can pay by local
transfer or card. A UK business asked to wire money to a personal Pakistani account will
simply stop replying.

**7. Deliver when you said you would.**
"Tomorrow" that arrives in two days is your first reliability signal, and it is a bad one.

**8. Silence is not rejection, and it is not an invitation to chase.**
No "Hii". No "Sir, waiting for your response..". No memes about waiting. Wait the
scheduled interval, then return once with something new. Then stop.

**9. Never claim a client you do not have.**
Spec work is spec work. Saying "an ad we made for a client" collapses the moment anyone
asks which client.

**10. Two price books until you have testimonials.**
Warm leads already in a thread: $80/video, $200 for three, and ask for a written
testimonial on delivery. Every new prospect from the new domain: $350, no discount.

## I. Portfolio presentation

**Do:** one link, to your own domain. `qzilabs.com/work`. A single page, three videos, autoplay muted, each captioned with one line about what it is.

**Link format (ALI's rule, 17 Sep):** never paste the raw URL. Always hide it behind short anchor text, e.g. "Watch a 20 second example". In Gmail: type the phrase, highlight it, press Ctrl+K, paste the link. Until the website exists, the Drive link sits behind that text.

**Do not:** Google Drive links (Gmail flags them, business networks block them), video attachments (spam risk on first contact), three separate links (choice paralysis), or a PDF (nobody opens a PDF from a stranger).

**Before any of it goes out:**
1. Re-render Erba Pura with the label correct, or cut every shot where text is legible
2. Retire `Portfolio video-4.mp4` entirely. 480p with @COLORCASEUSA on it cannot be shown
3. Crop Velara from 4K landscape to 9:16 vertical
4. Replace the QZI end card with a 1 second fade
5. Label each clearly as a spec piece, not a client job

## J. Deliverability checklist

Click by click, in order. Do not send cold email until step 7 is done.

1. Buy `qzilabs.com` (Namecheap or Cloudflare, about $10 to $15/yr)
2. Google Workspace or Zoho Mail on that domain, about $6/mo
3. In DNS settings add the **SPF** record Workspace gives you
4. Turn on **DKIM** in Workspace admin, paste the key into DNS
5. Add a **DMARC** record: `v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:you@qzilabs.com`
6. Verify all three at `mxtoolbox.com` and `dmarcian.com/dmarc-inspector`. All must pass
7. **Warm the domain 3 to 4 weeks.** Start at 5 emails/day to real people who reply. Build to 25/day. Instantly or Smartlead automate this
8. Test the exact template at `mail-tester.com`. Aim for 9/10 or better
9. Send to a friend's Gmail AND a friend's Outlook. Confirm **Inbox**, not Promotions
10. Cap at 25 to 30/day from one inbox. Never rotate accounts to dodge limits
11. Remove every bounced address the same day. Keep bounce rate under 3%
12. Keep the one line opt out in every email and honour it immediately

**Know the difference:**
- *Accepted* = their server took it. Says nothing about where it went.
- *Delivered* = it was filed somewhere. Could be Spam.
- *Inbox* = actually visible. Only seed tests tell you this.
- *Opened* = pixel fired. Unreliable, Apple Mail Privacy inflates it badly.
- *Replied* = the only metric that matters.

## L. Tracking

Use `QZI-tracker-v2.csv`. Diagnostic logic:

| Symptom | Investigate |
|---|---|
| High bounce rate | List quality and address sourcing |
| Delivered, zero opens, zero replies | Deliverability. Run seed tests again |
| Opens but no replies | Offer, CTA, or targeting |
| Replies but no sales | Price, trust, scope, or your sales process |
| "Too expensive" repeatedly | Price, or you are pitching brands with no budget |
| "Not right now" repeatedly | Timing. Target brands with live launches |

No single metric is conclusive. Open rate especially is unreliable.

## Appendix: on batch testing different price ranges

You asked to send batches at different prices. Two problems with doing it in the cold email:

1. **It tests the wrong stage.** Your bottleneck is a 0.2% reply rate. Price affects whether someone *buys*, not whether they *reply*. Optimising price now is tuning a stage you never reach.
2. **You cannot read the result at your volume.** Telling a 1% reply rate apart from a 2% one needs several hundred sends *per arm*. v1 cycled four prices per prospect, which produced noise, not data.

**Do this instead:** no price in the first email. Quote $350 in the reply. Log every price reaction in the tracker's `objection` column. After 20 real conversations you will know what the market says, from people who actually engaged, which is worth more than a thousand silent sends.

If you still want a cold-email price test after that: two arms only, $350 vs $250, 250 sends each, split randomly within the same segment, nothing else different between them.
