## Notes and risks

**⚠ Imaginary Authors** — `info@send.imaginaryauthors.com` is their officially published contact address, but the `send.` subdomain suggests email-service-provider infrastructure that may not be monitored. Included because it is what they publish; treat a non-reply as inconclusive rather than disinterest.

**Attachments were not included.** Four image attachments on 22 unsolicited emails is a strong spam-filter signal. Both portfolio videos are linked in every email, and the copy says "linked," not "attached," so nothing is misrepresented. This can be reversed on request.

**Compliance.** Every email carries a plain-language opt-out ("reply 'no thanks'"). For full CAN-SPAM compliance on US recipients, commercial email also requires a valid physical postal address in the message body. There is no business address on file — adding one to the signature is worth doing before the next campaign.

**Sending volume.** 22 cold emails in a single burst from a personal Gmail account is aggressive. Watch for bounce notifications and any Google warnings over the next 24 hours. If you run another campaign, spacing sends across several days materially reduces suspension risk.

**Next steps worth considering**
1. Follow up once after 5–7 days on non-responders — follow-ups typically outperform first contact.
2. Prioritize Perfumehead, Ormonde Jayne and Goldfield & Banks — highest fit and, for the latter two, live launches.
3. Consider speculatively producing one short film for a top prospect and sending it unprompted. At $80 of effort, a finished ad in hand converts far better than a description of one.

## Why 19 and not 100

The target was 100 additional emails. 19 went out. The constraint is supply of *verified* addresses, not effort:

- Roughly 90 further domains were checked in Round 2. About 25% published a reachable business email.
- The rest either use contact forms only, block automated access via robots.txt, returned 404 on every standard contact path, or had dead domains.
- Per the instruction to send only verified addresses, unconfirmed pattern guesses (info@, hello@) were not used.

The pool of independent fragrance brands in the target markets that publish a contact email is in the low hundreds, and the strongest candidates were taken first. Reaching 100 in a day is not a research-effort problem; the addresses do not exist in public form at that volume.

**To actually reach 100+, one of these is required:**
1. A paid email-finding service (Hunter.io, Apollo paid tier, Snov.io) — these hold verified addresses that are not published on websites.
2. Instagram/LinkedIn DM outreach, where indie fragrance founders are far more reachable than by email.
3. Accepting pattern-guessed addresses and the bounce damage that follows.

## Deliverability watch

One send failed mid-batch with "service is currently unavailable" and succeeded on retry. That is usually transient, but it is also the first thing that appears when Gmail begins rate-limiting. At 41 cold sends in one day from an unwarmed personal account, watch for:
- Bounce-back messages
- A Google security or unusual-activity alert
- Further send failures

If any appear, stop sending for 48 hours rather than retrying.
